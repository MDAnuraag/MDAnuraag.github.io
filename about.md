I'm a metrology process engineer at Samsung's Taylor fab. My degree is in microelectronics.

Inverse problems came to me through metrology. A forward model can be physically exact and still not tell you what is on the wafer, because the measurement doesn't pin the parameters down. Statistical optics came out of conversations at KLA and out of the discontinuities I kept running into in my research work. I'm building it slowly, on my own for now. If I go back to graduate school, I want formal training in it.

## Day to day

The fab is ramping copy exact, so the process comes from the Korea fab and I'm sorting measurement data from round robin wafers before production. Recipes are still owned in Korea and the local metrology compute isn't fully installed yet, so part of the job is knowing what I can see and what I can't.

Most of it is being comfortable with the tools: how the software handles a number before it reaches me, and whether that number means what it says. I got that from years of poking at my own machines and software. It matters more than people expect.

## Why this site exists

It's a reference for me. I like being able to pull up a piece of what I'm thinking. I use agents to help build and draft it, which is the only reason it gets kept up.

Most of what's here is projects that were handed to me, and what I did with them to get to where I am. Some of them failed. I'd rather leave the reasoning visible than clean it up.
