## Rule

A spectrum can constrain possible mechanisms.

It does not automatically identify a unique structure.

---

## Failure mode

A measured or computed spectrum matches a proposed model, so the model is treated as identified.

Wrong inference.

Correct inference:

```text
matching spectrum = consistency under assumptions
```

Not:

```text
matching spectrum = unique microscopic structure
```

Forward direction:

$$
\text{structure}
\rightarrow
\text{spectrum}
$$

Inverse direction:

$$
\text{spectrum}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{unique structure}
$$

---

## Why

Spectra compress information.

They may average over:

```text
depth
time
angle
grain structure
disorder
dopant environment
instrument bandwidth
preprocessing choices
```

After that compression, different microscopic states can look similar.

---

## Check

Before claiming structural identification:

```text
list alternative mechanisms
state which measurements distinguish them
separate "consistent with" from "identified as"
state what remains non-identifiable
```

If the distinguishing measurement is missing, the claim is consistency.
